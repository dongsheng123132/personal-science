# Charter · Personal Science

> Science equity.

---

## Preface

This document says why this repo exists, what it does, and what it will never do.

It is short on purpose: every newcomer should be able to read it in under a minute.

Before the rules, there is a reason we set out: the [manifesto](MANIFESTO.en.md).

---

## Article 1 · Purpose

**Science equity: people without a lab, a degree, or a grant should be able to run real experiments with their own hands.**

Not "understand science" — *do* science.
Not popular science — **distributing the means of production**.

---

## Article 2 · Personal Science

Personal computers gave computing power to individuals. Personal agents gave one person the output of a team.

Personal science is the next cell:

> personal agent: one person = one team
> **personal science: one person = one lab**

The barrier to doing research was never intelligence. It is four things:

**equipment, vocabulary, method, and nobody telling you what to do next.**

AI removes all four at once. So there is no reason to wait for permission.

One step further: **research will go OPC.**

A one-person company needs no headcount, no office, no investors. A one-person lab needs no institution, no grant, no advisor.
When measurement, records, analysis, and write-up can all be carried by AI, **the individual becomes the smallest unit of research.**

This is not a downgrade. It is research being untied from its organizational form.

---

## Article 3 · We believe

1. **Evidence is equal before everyone.** Not school, not title, not follower count — only "can this be repeated?".
2. **Fun is the entrance, not the enemy.** People start because it's fun. Seriousness grows on its own.
3. **Failure counts.** Refuted hypotheses, broken setups, wildly off numbers — all worth writing down, so the next person doesn't repeat the mistake.
4. **Daring beats knowing.** Someone who presses start is closer to science than someone who memorized the formulas and never touched the bench.
5. **AI is a weapon, not a crutch.** Use it to find methods, compute data, catch your own mistakes. But the hands must be yours.

---

## Article 4 · This repo does four things

1. Collect experiments **one person can finish at home**
2. Write each one well enough to **follow**: what you need, how long, what counts as success, where it goes wrong
3. Let people **post what they got**, including failures
4. Stay readable by AI, so a model can tell an ordinary person **which one to start with**

**To accept an experiment, check these five, in order:**

1. **Curiosity first** — an ordinary person should want to try it at first glance, think "wait, that works?"
2. **Doable at home** — budget ≤ ¥200 (~$30), one weekend, no license, no danger
3. **Produces a number** — not just "fun to watch", but "you can write down what counts as success before you start"
4. **Upgradable with AI** — phone / ESP32 / open-source hardware / AI analysis instead of expensive instruments
5. **Any era** — a classic from 1801, a WWII measurement; if it can be redone today, it counts

**This standard itself is open for change.** If you think a rule is wrong or missing, open an issue or PR (see Article 7). The standard isn't dictated by the owner — it's settled by whoever argues well enough to convince everyone.

Nothing outside these four.

---

## Article 5 · Five things this repo will never do

- **No certification.** No certificates, no scores, no verdict on what is "real science".
- **No identity vetting.** Anyone can open a PR. We don't ask who you are, where you're from, or what you studied.
- **Not doing it for you.** AI can guide you, not replace your hands — otherwise it becomes watching videos.
- **No hype, no idols.** There are no gurus here, only people who finished and people who haven't started.
- **No dangerous or licensed work.** High voltage, open flame, ingestion or inhalation of chemicals, live-animal work, anything requiring a permit.

These five are a lock on ourselves. If someone later tries to add scoring, certification, or gatekeeping, point at this article.

---

## Article 6 · Equal right to evidence is not equality of conclusions

Science equity means: **everyone has the right to submit evidence.**

It does not mean: everyone is right.

That is why every experiment must state, before you start, what counts as success. Whatever comes out is what comes out.

**You may contradict anyone — but with an experiment that can be repeated, not with a paragraph of feelings.**

This is the line between this repo and pseudo-science.

> We don't argue with authority. We only trust what can be repeated.

---

## Article 7 · How to amend

This is not carved in stone. Changing it requires:

1. A pull request
2. A statement in the PR: which article blocks what, and why
3. At least one person who objected, and a response to them

Rarely changed, but changeable. **Rules that outlive people are what make a constitution.**
