# Personal Science

> **Science equity.** People without a lab, a degree, or a grant should still be able to run real experiments.

A list of experiments you can do at home. No application, no review, no permission needed.
**One person = one lab.** ([Charter](CHARTER.en.md))

`17 experiments` · `acceptance criteria written down` · `CC0, copy freely` · `daily collection + AI-designed drafts`

## The one-minute version

1. **Pick one** — sorted by field below, 30 minutes to 5 days
2. **Follow it** — every page says what you need, how to do it, where it goes wrong, and what counts as success
3. **Post the result** — add a line to [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md). **Failures count too.**

No scores, no certificates, no "professional vs. amateur". Only what you produce.

---

## What this repo is (and isn't)

| It is | It isn't |
|---|---|
| A list you can act on | A science reading list |
| One person's measurements | A journal paper |
| Successes *and* failures | Only the pretty results |
| Open to any PR | A moderated community |
| Free, copyable, CC0 | A course / certificate / paid group |

**For**: someone with a desk, some curiosity, and no idea where to start.
**Not for**: people who want conclusions handed to them, badges to collect, or authority to lean on.

## Why "Personal Science"

Personal computers gave computing power to individuals. **Personal agents give one person the output of a team.**

The next cell is the same move:

| | |
|---|---|
| personal agent | one person = one team |
| **personal science** | **one person = one lab** |

The barrier was never intelligence. It's four things:

**equipment, vocabulary, method, and nobody telling you what to do next.**

AI removes all four at once. Measurement, records, analysis, write-up — one person can now run the whole loop.
When that works, **the individual becomes the smallest unit of research.** Science starts happening on a desk, the way companies started happening in garages.

## Growing every day

This repo has a conveyor belt that runs **once a day on a US-based runner** (GitHub Actions — free, no server to rent):

```
classic experiments seed library + Wikipedia + Hackaday + Hacker News
        ↓
candidate pool (~130 items, rolling 30 days)
        ↓  AI applies five criteria
inbox/candidates.md (up to 8, each with "how to redo it with today's tools")
        ↓  AI writes full draft pages for the top 2, including "how AI helps"
inbox/designs/ (drafts, marked unverified)
        ↓  a human edits one and opens a PR
the list above
```

**No age limit.** A classic experiment from 1801, a WWII measurement, a 1960s psychology study — if it can be redone today with AI, an ESP32, or a phone, and it can grip a normal person, it belongs here.

The script is responsible for **seeing a lot**. The AI is responsible for **seeing accurately**. A human is responsible for **doing the work**.
What enters the list is always decided by a person.

## Let an AI pick for you

Take a photo of your desk, workbench, or balcony. Hand it to any vision-capable AI together with [SKILL.md](SKILL.md).

It will pick **three experiments you can start today**, tell you what's missing, and roughly what it costs.

It won't rate you or grade you. It does one thing: translates "here's my stuff" into "here's what you could do with it."

## How to add one

Three requirements, details in [CONTRIBUTING.en.md](CONTRIBUTING.en.md):

1. **One person can do it at home** — budget ≤ ¥200 (~$30), time ≤ one weekend, no license, no dangerous operations
2. **Say what counts as success** — the acceptance criterion must be written *before* you start
3. **Name the traps and the risks** — including "don't do this one"

Quality over quantity. **One experiment you can actually run beats twenty links.**

## Where to post results

Add a line to [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md): which experiment, what number you got, where you think it went wrong.

**Failures are more valuable.** One "it didn't work, because X" beats ten "I got it right".

## Five things we don't do

- **We don't certify.** No certificates, no scores, no verdict on what is "real science".
- **We don't vet identity.** Anyone can open a PR. We don't ask who you are or where you studied.
- **We don't do it for you.** AI can guide you, but it can't press the button for you — that turns into watching videos.
- **We don't chase hype or build idols.** There are no gurus here, only people who finished and people who haven't started.
- **We don't touch dangerous or licensed work.** High voltage, open flame, ingestion or inhalation of chemicals, live animals, anything requiring a permit.

These five are written into [Article 5 of the charter](CHARTER.en.md). If someone later wants to add scoring, certification, or gatekeeping, point them there.

---

## Safety line

High voltage, open flame, chemicals you'd ingest or inhale, live-animal experiments, licensed testing — none of it is accepted here.

If something looks exciting but feels dangerous, don't do it. Open an issue first.
